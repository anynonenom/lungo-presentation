# Lungo × EIDEN — Final Story and Copy

## Narrative principle

The presentation must feel like a conversation with an owner who already has taste. It begins with respect, makes the gap easy to see, names a sharper position, turns that position into a creative system, and ends with a clear way to begin. Every slide carries **one idea, one memorable line, and one visual gesture**.

## Copy rules

Headlines must be understandable on the first read and strong enough to remember after the meeting. Body copy uses short sentences and familiar words. Strategic language is translated into visible consequences: what people see, ask for, remember, and return to. Darija is used as character, never decoration, and only where phrasing has already been supplied or approved.

## Presentation rhythm

The deck alternates between **photographic desire**, **strategic clarity**, and **operational proof**. No two consecutive slides should share the same composition. Cards are used only when the information truly behaves like a set; most slides rely on scale, contrast, image, and deliberate whitespace.
